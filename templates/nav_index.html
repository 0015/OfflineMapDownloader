    Offline Navigation Packager

    1 · Route

    ?

    Click the map to drop waypoints. Every one is a via point, so the route goes the way you drew it rather than the way the router would have preferred. Tick the loop box and it comes back to the start, and the device laps it instead of arriving.

    Avoid tolls / highways keeps the route off them wherever there is a reasonable way round, and says so on the route card when there is not. Toggle one after planning and the route is planned again, so you can compare the two.

    Walking and cycling routes use footpaths, park paths, steps, trails and cycleways that a car route never touches. Balanced is the router's own judgement. Prefer trails leans onto paths and trails even where that is a little longer; for cycling it plans for a mountain bike. Shortest takes every cut-through the mode is allowed, whatever the climb or surface. The last two also allow hiking trails up to mountain hiking (T2) on foot. Switching style after planning plans again, like the avoid switches.

    1 · Area

    Click two opposite corners on the map.

    ?

    For a map to look at, with no route and nothing to follow. Every tile the rectangle touches is downloaded at each zoom you pick, so keep it to the ground you need: the count grows with the area, and four times over for every zoom level you add.

    Drag a corner to adjust it; a third click starts a new rectangle. The device lists the tile folder beside the routes as a map of its own, and a card with nothing else on it opens straight into the map.

    2 · Tiles
    ?

    z16–17 is the sweet spot for driving; every extra level roughly doubles or triples a route's tile count. The corridor is how far either side of the route to keep tiles — wider means you can pan around, or stray off route, without hitting blank map.

    Settings

    Goes in the User-Agent the tile servers see. Not enforced by anything here, but it is what their policies ask for and what lets a provider reach you instead of just blocking you.

    Walking, cycling, and driving that avoids tolls or highways are planned by Valhalla: the OSRM demo only knows the car, and cannot leave roads out.

    ?

    Routes covering the same ground should use the same folder and will share its tiles, and so can an area. Give a route in another region its own, e.g. tiles_tahoe. Unzip several builds onto one card and the device lists them all.
